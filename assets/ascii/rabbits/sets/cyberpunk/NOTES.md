# Set: cyberpunk

Neon-rain noir. Six animations, all validated with `./check.sh` (exit 0).

Colour is never in the frames — it is applied at render time. Brightness is carried by glyph
weight only: `o` < `0` < `*` for a lit eye or lens, `-` < `=` for a live circuit, and a lit sign
is the presence of its letters rather than a different character.

| netrunner | 5 | 24x10 | 24x10 | 160 | yes | Bunny at a terminal, forepaws alternating on the keys, screen log scrolling, one dark flicker frame where its eyes blink |
| neon-rain | 6 | 36x12 | 36x12 | 200 | yes | Bunny on a wet street under sparse slanting rain, skyline behind, BUNNY sign flickering and reflecting in the puddle |
| trenchcoat | 4 | 24x10 | 24x10 | 220 | yes | Upright in a long coat, collar popped, hem swinging left-centre-right-centre; one slow blink |
| goggles | 4 | 24x10 | 24x10 | 180 | yes | Aviator goggles pushed up on the forehead, left lens pulsing `o` `0` `*` `0`; blink out of phase |
| chrome-ear | 4 | 24x10 | 24x10 | 140 | yes | One organic ear, one segmented prosthetic; a pulse climbs from the skull socket to the emitter cap |
| jack-in | 6 | 24x10 | 24x10 | 220 | **no** | Cable comes in from the right and seats at the temple; the ear stops flicking, the eyes flicker shut, then light. Hold frame 6 |

## Design rules this set follows

- **Neon by shape, not density.** Most of every canvas is empty. The sign is five characters, a
  glint is one, the street is a single hard `_` edge. No shading fills (`#`, `@`, `%`, `8`) appear
  anywhere in the set — the whole character inventory is line art plus the screen and sign text.
- **The bunny is the canonical sprite.** Curved skull, ears rising from the crown as a connected
  pair, one `v` muzzle below and between the eyes, closed base. Nothing here reinvents the rabbit.
- **Two head sizes, on purpose.** The three scene pieces (netrunner, neon-rain, trenchcoat) use the
  canonical 9-wide head so the bunny can share the canvas with a monitor or a skyline. The three
  portraits (goggles, chrome-ear, jack-in) use an 11-wide, 3-ear-row enlargement of the same head —
  same construction, just closer to camera. Both fill their canvas top to bottom.

## Rejected, and why

- **Netrunner in profile.** The screen glow has to land on the face, so the monitor must sit on the
  side the bunny faces; that forced a mirrored sprite, and one eye plus a side muzzle read worse at
  this size than the proven front view. Front view also matches `bun55` — the bunny at the laptop.
- **Netrunner with a full body sitting at the desk.** No canvas left for the terminal. What fixed
  it was `bun55` again: the body is *behind* the desk. Only head, forelegs and paws are drawn.
- **Netrunner paws as `_/` and `\_` merged into the head base.** A one-character change per frame
  read as noise, not typing. Replaced with `(_)` paws that bob a whole row between the keyboard and
  the raised position, plus a fifth frame with both paws lifted so the alternation never stutters
  across the loop point.
- **Netrunner monitor as a 5-row box with two blank interior lines.** Empty box, no information.
  Replaced with a 4-line log that scrolls one line per frame over a 5-line cyclic buffer, so the
  scroll returns exactly to its start after five frames.
- **Rain generated by modular arithmetic — twice.** `(r+c) % 5` put drops in repeating vertical
  columns. Adding a hash phase and `% 4` was worse: it lit whole *rows* at once
  (`/ /   / /   / /`), which reads as a dashed line, not weather. The cause is structural — one
  phase per diagonal means every diagonal sharing a phase fires on the same row. Replaced with 12
  hand-placed drop tracks; each repeats every 6 rows and moves one row down and one column left per
  frame, so the 6-frame loop still closes exactly and no two drops share a row nearby.
- **Trenchcoat hem where only the two end characters flipped `\` / `/`.** One character per frame,
  invisible in play. Replaced by translating the whole hem one column left / centre / right /
  centre — the fabric moves as a piece and the corner glyphs lean the way it is going.
- **Goggles lens flare drawn as a `'` above the lens.** `BRIEF.md` warns explicitly that
  apostrophes sprinkled round a static head read as artifacts rather than motion, and it did.
  Dropped; the lens pulse carries the glint alone, with a blink one frame out of phase for life.
- **Goggles on the canonical 9-wide head.** `(o)` twice plus a strap needs eight interior columns
  and the 9-wide head has five. That is why the portrait head was widened to 11 rather than
  shrinking the goggles into something that no longer read as two lenses.
- **Chrome-ear with a dark rest frame** (pulse, pulse, flash, dark). Too quiet — three of four
  frames differed by a single character in the same place. Using four *travel* positions instead
  (skull socket, lower segment, upper segment, emitter) makes the light visibly climb.

## Known limitations — stated rather than hidden

- **jack-in does not connect at the back of the head.** A front view has no visible back of the
  head, and a rear or profile view would hide the eyes, which is where the whole payoff is. The
  cable seats at the right temple, level with the crown. This is a deliberate reading of the ask,
  not an oversight.
- **chrome-ear's loop restarts the pulse at the base** instead of easing back down, so the step
  from frame 4 to frame 1 is larger than the steps inside the cycle. That is quality-bar point 4
  knowingly broken: a travelling pulse teleports by nature, and a fade-out frame made the animation
  read as broken rather than rhythmic. If it looks wrong in play, insert a fifth all-`-` frame.
- **Rain never crosses the bunny or the sign.** Both are masked with a one-column margin. Drops
  drawn over the sprite made the silhouette read as static, which is exactly what the brief warns
  about; the cost is a visible rain-free column around the bunny on close inspection.
- **neon-rain needs 36 columns and 12 rows** and has no smaller fallback. A player must check the
  window and fall back to one of the 24x10 pieces.
- **The trenchcoat carries no cyberpunk marker beyond the coat itself.** A belt, a lapel pin or a
  cuff seam were all considered and all cost more characters than they returned at this size. It
  reads as a rabbit in a long coat with the collar up, which is the whole ask, but it is the
  quietest piece in the set.

## Calls made where the task was ambiguous

- "24 wide x 10 tall for character pieces" was read as an exact canvas, not a maximum: every 24x10
  piece uses all ten rows, and neon-rain uses all of 36x12. No piece has dead margin rows.
- Screen and sign text is ASCII words (`> ice`, `BUNNY`). That is not "colour" and not a generator
  — it is hand-placed, and a terminal with nothing on it does not read as a terminal.
- The sign says `BUNNY` rather than invented signage, so a palette change and a project rename stay
  in one place.
