# 4.1 — snapshot byte-cap: proven (scoped), with named edges

**Question:** does the configured snapper system actually bound the disk
snapshots can consume (the author's hard requirement, 2026-08-23: "backups
should have a maximum amount of space they can take up so they don't blow up
the rest of the hard drive")?

**Scoped answer (survived DA round 2, see `da-logs/4.1.snapshot-cap.md`):**
`SPACE_LIMIT`/`FREE_LIMIT` with floor-0 range limits enforce an **eventual**
byte-cap on **number-class** snapshots. Error direction is **over-deletion**
(never under): btrfs keeps dying snapshots' qgroups (`<under deletion>`)
counted in `1/0` until the cleaner thread finishes, so the evaluator can only
see too much, not too little. Worst-case unenforced window ≈ `NUMBER_MIN_AGE`
(1800 s) + `snapper-cleanup.timer` cadence (daily) ≈ ~1 day.

Machine: `arch-bunny` (fresh 2026-08-23 install), snapper 0.13.1, btrfs on
LUKS2, fs size 249.4 GiB. Logs: `~/log/4.1.snapshot-cap-rerun.log` on the box.

## Production config (live, and what install.sh must write)

```
NUMBER_CLEANUP=yes  NUMBER_LIMIT=0-15  NUMBER_LIMIT_IMPORTANT=0-5
NUMBER_MIN_AGE=1800  TIMELINE_CREATE=no
SPACE_LIMIT=0.08   # ~19.9 GiB of this fs
FREE_LIMIT=0.2
QGROUP=1/0         # written by `snapper setup-quota`
+ snap-pac (pre/post pairs, cleanup=number) + snapper-cleanup.timer enabled
```

**⚠ Floor changed from the author-approved "2-15" to "0-15" during DA round 1**
— with a nonzero floor, the newest N snapshots are exempt from the cap, so two
sufficiently large snapshots blow it forever (DA r1 #1). Floor 0 is the
faithful reading of the requirement: under space pressure, *all* number-class
history is expendable. **Needs the author's ratification.**

## Round 1 (250 MB threshold) — what it taught

- First run deleted nothing: **`NUMBER_MIN_AGE` (default 1800 s) filters young
  snapshots even under space pressure** — the canary was seconds old. The test
  was null, not the mechanism.
- With min-age 0: the 1 GiB canary was deleted, small ones kept. DA rejected
  it — count-floor (2) and space-satisfaction coincided, indistinguishable.
- Two snapshots never joined qgroup 1/0 and a manual `btrfs qgroup assign`
  was silently reverted. Root cause read from snapper source, not guessed:
  `Snapper.cc prepareQuota()` — *"ensure that those and only those with a
  cleanup algorithm are included in the high level qgroup"* — runs at the
  start of every space-aware cleanup. **No-cleanup snapshots are exempt from
  the cap by design.** The pre-first-boot `baseline` snapshot is therefore
  uncapped; its pinned divergence grew 1.79 → 8.06 MiB during this test
  alone. Policy consequence: never leave no-cleanup snapshots on a BunnE
  machine unless consciously pinning them.

## Round 2 rerun (22 GiB at the production limit) — raw results

Load at start 0.23; at end 6.86 (the `dd` churn — durations below are
durations, no contention claim). Three urandom canaries (7+7+8 GiB, each
pinned exclusively by one snapshot), plus the real snap-pac pre/post pair
(#6/#7) from the desktop-set pacman transaction.

| stage | 1/0 exclusive | free (est) | snapshots (number-class) |
|---|---|---|---|
| before cleanup | 22.00 GiB (> 19.93 limit) | 239.14 GB | #6 #7 #8(7G) #9(7G) #10(8G) |
| after `cleanup number` (5.25 s) | 8.00 GiB | 254.17 GB (+14 GiB) | #10 only |
| after FREE_LIMIT=0.999 + cleanup (1.34 s) | 0 | 262.76 GB (+8 GiB) | none |

- **Space-driven stop proven:** floor was 0, yet cleanup stopped with #10
  alive (8.00 < 19.93). A floor cannot explain stopping; only the space
  condition can. snap-pac's #6/#7 auto-joined 1/0 with zero manual action.
- **Real bytes returned:** +22 GiB total free-space delta (`btrfs fi usage`,
  not qgroup self-report), matching the canary total.
- **Over-deletion observed:** #9 died although arithmetic said ~15 GiB ≤
  limit after #8. Mechanism (sourced at both ends, causal attribution still
  inferred): `BtrfsUtils.cc quota_rescan()` does `RESCAN` **then
  `RESCAN_WAIT`** (no stale rescan), but deleted snapshots' qgroups linger
  `<under deletion>` in 1/0 until the btrfs cleaner drops them — phantom
  bytes that only inflate. Over-deletes, never under-deletes.
- **Stop-loop read, not assumed:** `client/cleanup.cc Cleaner::cleanup(condition)`
  — `while (!condition())` → candidates beyond `Range::MIN`, oldest-first,
  pre/post as pairs → remove → re-evaluate `is_quota_satisfied()`
  (= 1/0 exclusive vs statvfs × SPACE_LIMIT, from `queryQuotaData()`).

## Named edges (all on the DA record)

1. No-cleanup snapshots are uncapped **by design** (baseline included).
2. Overshoot magnitude is workload-dependent (bytes pending the cleaner at
   evaluation); observed as one whole 7 GiB snapshot here.
3. FREE_LIMIT fired only at a synthetic 0.999 threshold on the production
   fs. **Addendum, same night (not separately DA-gated; loop-fs scope
   stated):** on a 10 GiB loopback btrfs with the SHIPPING value
   (FREE_LIMIT=0.2, SPACE_LIMIT deliberately unsatisfiable at 0.9,
   floor 0), filling the live fs to 11.4% free made `cleanup number`
   delete the oldest pinned snapshot and STOP exactly when free crossed
   back over 20% (21.2%) — free-driven, correct order, correct stop.
   `instruments/4.7.free-limit-loop.sh`, `raw/4.7.free-limit-loop.log`.
   Production-scale intervention still unwatched.
4. IMPORTANT-class cleanup untested (no such snapshots exist; snap-pac
   doesn't create them unless configured).
5. Concurrency (snap-pac create racing timer cleanup) untested; timer-unit
   execution environment unobserved (all runs were CLI).
6. n=2 runs, 2 scales, 1 install, 1 kernel/btrfs/snapper build.
