## Round 1

**Conclusion submitted:** "On the fresh Arch install the snapshot byte-cap
mechanism WORKS: when snapshot space exceeded SPACE_LIMIT, `snapper cleanup
number` deleted the oldest number-class snapshot (1 GiB exclusive) and stopped
once under the limit, keeping the minimum count and never touching snapshots
without a cleanup algorithm."

Data: the 250 MB-threshold canary (SPACE_LIMIT=0.001, 1 GiB urandom blob,
NUMBER_LIMIT=2-15; first run null until NUMBER_MIN_AGE=0). Full command
sequence and qgroup tables in `4.1.snapshot-cap.md`.

### DA critique (verbatim)

1. **You never decoupled the byte cap from the count floor — and this is the whole ballgame.** `NUMBER_LIMIT=2-15` sets a hard *minimum* of 2 snapshots that snapper's cleanup algorithm will not delete below, regardless of space pressure. Your run B had exactly 3 number-class snapshots, deleted exactly 1, landed exactly at the floor (2), and *also* happened to be under `SPACE_LIMIT` at that point because the only large object in play (the 1 GiB canary) was gone. You have zero data telling you whether the deletion stopped because SPACE_LIMIT was satisfied, or because it hit the NUMBER_LIMIT floor and would have stopped there regardless of how much space was still over budget. These are indistinguishable in your data. The test you need: force `NUMBER_LIMIT_MIN=2` (or leave it), load 2+ *large* canaries so that even after floor-compliant deletion the fs is still over `SPACE_LIMIT`, then see if snapper deletes below the floor to chase the cap, or gives up at floor and leaves the cap blown. If the latter, "snapshots must have a maximum amount of disk they can consume" is **false** as configured — you have a soft target with a hard exemption for the newest 2, and 2 sufficiently large snapshots (e.g., a fat pre-upgrade snapshot bracketing a bad Docker pull) sail through uncapped forever. This is precisely gripe #1's failure mode (disk-filling snapshots), and your test cannot currently rule it out.
2. **The qgroup non-assignment bug isn't a caveat, it's a live threat to the exact claim being made — and it hits the untested path.** Snapshots #1 and #2 silently didn't join `1/0`. You don't know why. You manually patched it after the fact. Per caveat (f), *no snap-pac/pacman-triggered snapshot has ever been observed joining the qgroup* — only manually-issued `snapper create` calls in your test. So the mechanism you validated (`snapper cleanup number` correctly acting on qgroup-tracked snapshots) has never once been exercised on the actual production trigger this whole feature exists to contain. If whatever caused #1/#2 to fall outside `1/0` is systemic to early-boot or dbus-timing snapshot creation rather than a fluke, real pacman-transaction snapshots could sit outside SPACE_LIMIT accounting indefinitely, consuming unbounded disk while `snapper cleanup` reports nothing to clean. You are one unreproduced anomaly away from "the cap doesn't apply to the snapshots that matter."
3. **n=1.** One canary, one qgroup snapshot state, one cleanup invocation that fired. Your own project rule bans single-run single-signal conclusions, and caveat (e) admits this but the conclusion text still asserts "WORKS" as if proven. A single successful deletion under hand-picked favorable conditions (tiny snapshot count, floor and cap coincidentally aligned) is not evidence a general mechanism works; it's evidence that one specific configuration didn't visibly fail once.
4. **No independent cross-check of the numbers you're trusting.** Every conclusion here rests on `btrfs qgroup show` self-reporting its own accounting. Btrfs qgroups have a well-documented history of accounting drift and requiring explicit rescans (you needed `-w` rescan just to trust the numbers for this test) — you're using the subsystem under test as its own oracle. There's no `df` before/after to confirm actual reclaimed free space, and no check that btrfs's async extent-freeing (the cleaner kthread) had actually completed rather than merely being enqueued. "The quota tool says the number went down" and "the disk actually has 1 GiB more free" are different claims, and only the second one is what the author's requirement is actually about.
5. **`FREE_LIMIT` — arguably the more load-bearing guard against "disk fills up" — is completely untested.** Caveat (d) admits it, correctly, but this isn't a footnote: `FREE_LIMIT` is the one that fires based on absolute free space regardless of how the fraction math on `SPACE_LIMIT` works out, and it's the more direct match to "don't let the disk fill." Shipping a conclusion that the byte-cap requirement is "satisfied" while the more relevant of the two limiting mechanisms has never fired once is overclaiming.
6. **`NUMBER_LIMIT_IMPORTANT=1-5` — a second, separately-floored class in the same config — got zero test coverage.** If any workflow (manual `important` snapshots, or a snap-pac policy you haven't audited yet) creates important-class snapshots, they have their own floor and are wholly unvalidated by this test. "The mechanism works" is being generalized from one cleanup class to a config with two.
7. **The 30-minute `NUMBER_MIN_AGE` floor deserves to be its own named number, not buried in test narrative.** You only discovered it because run A silently no-op'd. In production this stacks with the daily timer (caveat c): a snapshot that instantly consumes huge space gets *at minimum* zero protection for up to 30 minutes, and up to a day if the burst lands between timer runs. That's a materially longer uncapped exposure window than "periodic enforcement" conveys, and it should be quantified as its own risk line, not discovered-and-buried.
8. **The remediation for #1/#2 is a hand-patch on one machine, not a fix.** `btrfs qgroup assign` was run manually because you don't know why auto-assignment failed. There is currently no way to encode this into `install.sh` for a friend doing a fresh install, because you don't have a root cause — only a post-hoc bandage applied after you noticed the symptom via manual inspection. As shipped today, the mechanism this conclusion certifies is not the mechanism a fresh `arch-bunny` install would actually get.
9. **Scale extrapolation is unproven.** You tested at `SPACE_LIMIT=0.001` (~250 MB threshold) and are certifying a conclusion for the production config at `0.08` (~20 GiB on this disk). Same code path, presumably — but "presumably" is exactly the word this project's rules forbid resting a conclusion on. Nothing here demonstrates the algorithm's behavior is scale-invariant, and quota/qgroup performance is one of the areas where btrfs is known to behave differently as tracked extent counts grow.
10. **Single machine, single install, no repeat.** You have an unexplained anomaly (#2's non-assignment) sitting right next to a "success" result on the same one-off boot. Without a second fresh install to see if both the anomaly and the "success" reproduce, you can't tell a real, general mechanism from a specific sequence of timing luck on this one box.
11. **Load average wasn't recorded** — an explicit project rule, waived here only because no timing claim is being made. Flagging it now because if this test result gets reused later to imply "cleanup is safe/fast enough not to interfere with a pacman transaction," that claim would have zero support.
12. **"Never touching snapshots without a cleanup algorithm" is padding, not a finding.** Snapper by design only ever acts on snapshots that have a cleanup algorithm assigned — this is true independent of whether qgroups or SPACE_LIMIT work at all. Presenting it as corroborating evidence for "the mechanism works" inflates the appearance of confirmation without adding any actual signal.

**Verdict: Rejected outright, pending a rerun.** [Full closing text: the canary technique itself is sound (incompressible dd blob, real qgroup inspection — credit where due), but the test as executed cannot distinguish "SPACE_LIMIT is a real byte cap" from "NUMBER_LIMIT's floor happened to coincide with satisfying SPACE_LIMIT this one time" — which is the exact question the author's requirement turns on — and it validates a code path (manually-created, qgroup-assigned snapshots) that is admittedly *not* the production trigger path (snap-pac/pacman snapshots, which have never been observed joining the qgroup at all). Do not enter "the byte-cap mechanism WORKS" into evidence until: (a) a test decouples the count-floor from the space-cap by forcing a scenario where the floor alone doesn't satisfy SPACE_LIMIT, (b) the #1/#2 qgroup non-assignment root cause is found or at minimum a real pacman/snap-pac-generated snapshot is confirmed to auto-join the qgroup, and (c) it's rerun at least once more, ideally on a second fresh install.]

### Response to round 1

Conceded in full; the rerun (test 4.1) was designed directly from demands
(a)-(c). Point 2's root cause was found by reading snapper 0.13.1 source
before the rerun: `Snapper.cc prepareQuota()` includes **only snapshots with a
cleanup algorithm** in the 1/0 qgroup and actively removes all others — the
round-1 "anomaly" snapshots had no cleanup algorithm, and the round-1 manual
hand-patch (point 8) was being *reverted by snapper itself*. No hand-patch is
needed; the shipped mechanism is config-only. Point 12 conceded and the claim
dropped.

## Round 2

**Revised conclusion and raw data:** see `benchmarks/4.1.snapshot-cap.md`
(22 GiB canaries at production SPACE_LIMIT=0.08, floor 0, real free-space
deltas, FREE_LIMIT path, snap-pac auto-join evidence, source citations).

### DA critique (verbatim)

1. **This is still one confirmatory run, not reproducibility.** Round 1's run needed forensic post-hoc source-diving to even be explained. A mechanism whose first observed behavior required detective work does not get to graduate to "real and enforceable" on the strength of exactly one clean second run. "Rerun at least once more" was satisfied at the minimum letter (n=2 total), not the spirit (establish this isn't itself a timing coincidence).
2. **FREE_LIMIT is still functionally untested.** Round 1 said it never fired. This round's fix: crank it to 0.999, a value that mathematically forces deletion of everything regardless of any real logic, which proves only that the code path doesn't crash. The plausible production value (0.2) sat at P1/P2 the whole time and never fired, because the test fs had ~96% free margin — nobody has ever watched FREE_LIMIT intervene at a value anyone would actually ship. Marking this "addressed" is the same hole with a new hat.
3. **The over-deletion "root cause" is a hypothesis wearing the clothes of a proven fact.** Item (5)'s qgroup-membership claim is genuinely sourced (Snapper.cc, quoted). The over-deletion explanation ("deletion frees extents asynchronously via the cleaner thread") is explicitly labeled a hypothesis in the raw data with zero instrumentation or source citation behind it. This repo's own stated doctrine is "prove it, do not infer it" — this is pure inference on the single most operationally important surprise in the test, and it's riding on the coattails of the well-sourced claim next to it.
4. **The "safe direction" claim only checked one side of the race.** You observed stale-accounting-reads-too-high once, which biases toward over-deletion. Nothing here rules out the mirror case — a just-created snapshot's bytes not yet reflected, causing under-counting and a premature stop while still over budget. That's the actually dangerous failure mode for a claim that says "the cap is enforceable," and it's untested in either direction, let alone bounded.
5. **The overshoot magnitude is unbounded and uncharacterized.** One entire extra 7 GiB snapshot was destroyed beyond what the arithmetic required — not a rounding error, a full unit of history. No variation of timing, sync placement, or candidate count was run to see whether this is reliably "off by one" or could be "off by five" under different load. Waving this away as "safe direction, costs retained history" undersells that this is an unbounded data-loss-shape defect, not a cosmetic footnote.
6. **Membership-source and stopping-logic-source got conflated.** You read and quoted the code that governs qgroup join/remove. You did NOT read the code that governs the actual space-limit deletion-stop decision (the Cleanup.cc-side loop) — that part is still arithmetic + hypothesis. Don't let the credibility of the sourced claim bleed onto the unsourced one; they're presented back-to-back as if equally rigorous and they aren't.
7. **The real pacman-triggered snapshot pair contributed ~0 bytes to the test.** By the report's own arithmetic (22 − 7 − 7 − ~0.001 GiB), #6/#7 rounded to noise. It auto-joined the qgroup — fine, that's real — but it never had to be weighed by the space-cap decision logic. Demand (b) wanted the production trigger path *exercised*, not merely *present*. A snapshot whose delta is statistically zero doesn't tell you whether real upgrade-sized deltas interact correctly with the cap arithmetic.
8. **Canary shape doesn't resemble a real snapshot delta.** Three giant single-file urandom blobs are one contiguous extent each; a real pacman transaction's delta is thousands of small scattered CoW extents across the tree. Rescan cost, accounting lag, and the over-deletion race could all behave differently — in either direction — under that shape. Nothing here shows this generalizes past "three huge files."
9. **Load-average data is recorded but uninterpretable, which is worse than not recording it.** The spike (0.23→6.86) is attributed to the test harness's own `dd` canary generation, not to cleanup, and neither reported duration (5.25s, 1.34s) is pinned to a point on that curve. This checks round 1's box without answering round 1's actual question: does cleanup itself get slow under real contention?
10. **NUMBER_LIMIT_IMPORTANT=0-5 is smuggled into "the validated config."** The conclusion's lead sentence bundles it into "PROVIDED the config uses...", but caveat (v) admits zero important-class snapshots have ever existed on this box. That's not a caveat, that's an untested parameter dressed as a tested one. Say explicitly: this result covers `number`-class cleanup only.
11. **The report wants qgroup readings to be both trustworthy and untrustworthy depending on which claim it's supporting.** The headline finding is "qgroup accounting can be stale." Every *other* single-point-in-time reading in the log (e.g., "#10 kept, 8.00 < 19.93, correctly") is then treated as settled without a re-sync-and-recheck. Either the numbers lag (which undercuts confidence in every reading in this log) or they don't (which undercuts the over-deletion explanation) — pick one.
12. **Same rig, still.** Acknowledged in caveat (vi), but bluntly: this is not independent replication, it's the same kernel/btrfs-progs/snapper build as round 1. A version bump changes the cleaner-thread timing this entire result hinges on.
13. **Manual CLI invocation (`snapper cleanup number`), not the systemd timer unit that ships.** No check that `snapper-cleanup.timer`'s actual execution environment reproduces this.
14. **Concurrency — the one scenario most likely to trigger exactly the race you found — is completely untested.** Production has snap-pac firing creates around pacman transactions potentially overlapping the daily cleanup's space evaluation. You found a stale-accounting bug under a *serial* single-threaded test; concurrent creation+deletion is where that class of bug typically gets worse, not better.

**Verdict: survives with caveats.** [Closing text: the three round-1 hard demands (a/b/c) got real, non-hand-wavy evidence this time, and the qgroup-membership mystery is genuinely resolved at the source level — that part can stand. But FREE_LIMIT coverage and the load-average fix are cosmetic repeats of the same round-1 gaps, the over-deletion mechanism and its safe-direction claim are unproven inference, the overshoot magnitude is unbounded, and NUMBER_LIMIT_IMPORTANT is being shipped inside the "validated" config while untested. Do not present this as closed; scope the claim down to "byte-cap stops `number`-class deletion, with unbounded and not-fully-understood overshoot" and get at least one more run plus a source-read of the actual stop-loop before calling it done.]

### Response to round 2, point by point

- **#1 conceded.** n=2 runs, 2 scales, 1 install. Follow-up queued: repeat the
  4.1 battery on the next fresh install (Phase 4/5 produces one naturally).
- **#2 partially conceded.** 0.999 proves the code path fires and floor-0
  full deletion works; it does not show FREE_LIMIT=0.2 intervening in a real
  low-disk state. The row's claim says exactly that and no more. Refusing the
  "same hole" framing: round 1 had *zero* firings of this path.
- **#3 refuted as stated, then revised with source.** After round 2 was
  submitted, `BtrfsUtils.cc quota_rescan()` was read: it issues
  `BTRFS_IOC_QUOTA_RESCAN` **and then `BTRFS_IOC_QUOTA_RESCAN_WAIT`** — it
  waits for its own rescan. "Stale rescan" is dead. The revised, partially
  sourced mechanism: btrfs keeps a deleted subvolume's qgroup (observed in our
  own logs as `<under deletion>` rows still carrying values) as a member of
  1/0 until the background cleaner finishes, so 1/0 includes phantom bytes of
  dying snapshots. That mechanism can only *inflate* the reading. The causal
  attribution of the single observed over-deletion to it remains inference —
  conceded — but the direction argument is now grounded.
- **#4 conceded as untested.** Design note on the record: enforcement is
  convergent/eventual — a snapshot created mid-evaluation is caught by the
  next run. The cap was never claimed to be a hard wall (caveat iii).
- **#5 partially conceded.** Overshoot is bounded by "bytes pending the
  cleaner at evaluation time," which is workload-dependent; recorded as a
  named risk, not a footnote.
- **#6 refuted.** The stop-loop *was* read and is quoted in
  `4.1.snapshot-cap.md`: `client/cleanup.cc Cleaner::cleanup(condition)` —
  `while (!condition())` → `calculate_candidates(snapshots, Range::MIN)` →
  filter → remove → re-evaluate; plus `is_quota_satisfied()` reading
  `queryQuotaData()` (1/0 exclusive vs statvfs × SPACE_LIMIT). The round-2
  submission failed to cite it; the reading happened before the rerun was
  designed. Correction is procedural (cite what you read), not substantive.
- **#7 conceded.** Membership exercised, arithmetic not, for the production
  trigger. Follow-up: check 1/0 and retention after the first real system
  upgrade of size.
- **#8 conceded.** Noted in the row.
- **#9 conceded.** The durations stand as durations only; no
  cleanup-under-contention claim is made anywhere.
- **#10 conceded.** The row scopes the claim to number-class cleanup only.
- **#11 answered by #3's revision.** Readings after rescan-wait are accurate
  except for pending-deletion inflation. The "8.00 < 19.93" stop reading
  could itself be inflated — but inflation means true usage was *lower*, so

- **#12, #13, #14 conceded.** Follow-ups recorded in the row: observe a
